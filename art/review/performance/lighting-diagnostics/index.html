Measured conclusion

Spotlight lighting and per-pixel shadow processing are the primary confirmed costs. In the apartment, removing spotlight shadows reduces whole-frame time from 95.6 ms to 61.5 ms; removing the spotlights reduces it to 40.8 ms. Removing fog changes it by only 0.6 ms. The courtyard baseline varied from 5.80 to 6.46 FPS; fog-off measured 6.53 FPS, so the small apparent fog benefit there is within baseline variation.

Disabling shadow receivers gives a much larger benefit than disabling casters, especially indoors: the main optimization target is per-pixel lighting/shadow work. Reducing triangle counts or caching shadow-map drawing alone would leave much of that work. A future image-preserving shader optimization could skip calculations proven to contribute zero, but its speed and image equivalence have not been tested here.

The diagnostic baseline apartment capture was pixel-identical to the original full-resolution capture.

Why small assets still cost so much

  • The full instanced GLB contains only 38,142 triangles (22,955 unique mesh triangles). Of its 78 embedded images, 48 are exactly 256 × 256; only one is 1024 × 1024, and two are 512 × 512.
  • Lighting is evaluated over millions of visible pixels. Six overlapping spotlights each have a 7 m range and a 1.3 radian outer half-angle (approximately a 149° full cone), alongside one shadow-casting directional light.
  • Bevy's Gaussian shadow filter performs nine comparison-sampler calls per relevant directional/spotlight shadow lookup. These generated shadow textures are 2048 × 2048 each, independent of the imported material texture sizes.
  • The default PBR shader fetches spotlight shadows and evaluates the light contribution before multiplying them together. Even contributions that end up blocked can incur both types of work.
  • Fog here is exponential-squared distance fog, not volumetric ray marching. The fog-only removal can be compared directly with the surrounding baseline measurements above.

The receiver/caster split distinguishes per-pixel shadow processing from drawing the shadow geometry. Removing receivers keeps the shadow maps rendering; removing casters retains the lookup code but samples clear maps. These are whole-frame comparisons, not GPU timestamp measurements. Differences between variants are not generally additive, and the caster test also changes shadow-texture contents.

The exploratory short runs included shader/timing stalls and are not used for this table. They suggested that smaller shadow maps and cheaper filtering alone had little benefit; those settings need longer confirmation before quoting gains.
